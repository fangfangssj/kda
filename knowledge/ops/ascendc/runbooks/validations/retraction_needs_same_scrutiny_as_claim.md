# OL-307 — OL-307: A5Ops additive extension

## 适用场景

- 适用平台与范围：soc=all; cann=all; op_class=all (reporting discipline)。
- 验证来源：four retractions in one multi-agent session, 2026-07-31; two of them shipped fresh errors that passed every review gate。

**OL-307** — **A retraction needs the same scrutiny as the claim it retracts — every sentence in it except the admission is a NEW assertion, and it rides free because a correction reads as already-reviewed.** Four retractions in one session, two shipping fresh errors past every gate (2026-07-31). (a) The retraction **moved a citation from one unverified thing to another** ("cite the measurement, not my reasoning" — but that measurement's relevance was equally unestablished; correct state was *neither is citable*). (b) The retraction **fabricated credit never extended**: "two peers credited me with the mechanism" — checking who each second-person pronoun addressed showed **both were addressed to someone else**, identifiable from the same paragraph ⇒ a pronoun IS a specific value. ⚠️ **Direction matters**: a self-unfavourable claim has no opponent (everyone agrees with self-criticism and the agreement feels like corroboration), so a self-FAVOURABLE claim hiding inside a self-criticism inherits that immunity — outwardly humble, substantively self-promoting, audited by nobody. (c) The retraction **inherited the shape of what it retracted** (a new whole-object cause asserted while withdrawing someone else's, refuted 15 min later); correcting others is the moment of lowest self-scrutiny — one agent narrowed a peer's scope and widened its own in the same message. **Rule**: check every non-admission sentence as a claim, hardest on "credit belongs to X"/"cite A not B"; never introduce a replacement cause in the same message as a withdrawal; keep "this action was a defect" and "this defect caused this failure" as two separately-retractable claims. **Corollary**: when someone diagnoses a failure as their own fault, that is the claim needing a CONTROL, not agreement — mistaken self-blame aims the repair at the wrong place and consumes the investigation slot the real cause needed.

**Principle**: a correction reads as though it has already been reviewed. The reader's
attention lands entirely on **the claim being withdrawn**, so every *other* assertion in the
retraction rides through unexamined — redirected citations, severity re-rankings, and above
all **claims about who found what**. Correction is not a privileged speech act.
**Instance A — the retraction moved a citation from one unverified thing to another.** An
author withdrew a wrong argument and wrote "cite the measurement instead of my reasoning."
But that measurement's *relevance to the failure* had never been established either (see
OL-306), and the real cause was found by a third party. The retraction looked like a
correction and was a lateral move; the correct state was **neither claim is citable as the
cause**.
**Instance B — the retraction fabricated credit that was never extended.** The same author
wrote "two peers credited me with supplying the mechanism," then checked who each second-person
pronoun addressed: **both were addressed to someone else**, identifiable from the same
paragraph in each case. Both halves false. ⇒ A pronoun **is a specific value**; who it points
at must be read, not assumed (OL-299's family: right format, wrong object).
⚠️ Note the direction. A self-*unfavourable* claim has no opponent — everyone agrees with
someone criticising themselves, and that agreement feels like corroboration. A self-*favourable*
claim **hiding inside** a self-criticism inherits that immunity while inflating the author's
contribution. Outwardly humble, substantively self-promoting, and audited by nobody.
**Instance C — the retraction inherited the shape of what it retracted.** Withdrawing a
peer's over-broad self-diagnosis, the corrector asserted a *different* whole-object cause in
the same message; it was refuted 15 minutes later. Correcting someone else is the moment of
lowest self-scrutiny: in the same session, an agent narrowing a peer's scope simultaneously
widened its own ("both need fixing", where the eventual answer was neither in the form
proposed).
**Rule**: (1) treat every sentence of a retraction except the admission itself as a new
claim, and check it accordingly; (2) apply this hardest to sentences of the form "credit
belongs to X" / "cite A not B"; (3) when withdrawing someone else's over-broad conclusion, do
not introduce a replacement cause in the same message — separate the acts; (4) prefer
"this action was a defect" and "this defect caused this failure" as **two** claims, so the
second can be withdrawn without withdrawing the first.
**Corollary for self-blame**: when a participant diagnoses a failure as their own fault, that
is the claim most in need of a **control experiment**, not the one most deserving of
agreement — and the cheapest control is to run something known to work, unmodified, in the
same place. Mistaken self-blame is not merely unfair; it **aims the repair at the wrong
place**, and it consumes the investigation slot the real cause needed.
